The writing itself was a quick creative practice piece I wrote after reading my way through the myth of Deucalion, wondering how it might show up in the inner turmoil and delusion of a dying man aboard an Atlantic voyage. It's a stiff piece, and I've left it mostly unedited from when it was written a few years back to preserve its raw integrity. The one change was the names, which now come from the myth: Lyall after Lycaon the wolf, Ruby after Pyrrha, and Seymour, the one who sees.

I first tested it as a Doré engraving, but it played like a slideshow, so I switched to oil painted on glass in the manner of Aleksandr Petrov. At first the video model attempted to unstylize the clips, leading to this weird morphing away from and back to the painted look. Restructuring the motion prompts and repainting the stills that had drifted, with the sea maiden's face as the reference for the brushwork, held the style. As seen in some weird jankiness, it's not perfect, but I am proud of the progress made.

## About the Work

A novel-length existential horror narrative steeped in Nietzschean concepts of Eternal Recurrence and the Will to Power. The story follows a survivor named **Menote** traversing a desolate landscape after a sudden global mass-death event. The narrative employs a **bicameral writing-style divergence** that mirrors the protagonist's psychological dissolution — as Menote's individuality gives way, his internal cognition is systematically replaced by personified states. Grief and destructive rage manifest as Homeric gods (*Oizys*, *Perses*) who actively dictate, override, and interact with his actions.

His journey leads to a cult-like group attempting to birth a technological deity — the Basilisk. The novel concludes with a recursive realization that Menote is trapped in an eternal cycle of trauma and rebirth, where the boundary between internal suffering and external cosmic horror collapses entirely.

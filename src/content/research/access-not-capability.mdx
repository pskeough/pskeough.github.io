## Overview

Can a lab answer questions over its own unpublished documents with a small model on its own hardware, or does it need a hosted frontier API? The study tests three decisions a lab makes: whether to use retrieval, whether to fine-tune, and whether a local model can judge the answers.

The primary corpus is 26 private research papers, with QASPER and COVID-QA as public replications. Local models at 1B, 3.8B and 8B parameters are compared against GPT-5.1, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and DeepSeek under a matched-evidence condition, where the frontier models receive the same retrieved passages as the local ones. Answers are judged blind in both presentation orders.

## Current results

- **Retrieval does most of the work.** Without retrieval, the frontier models win almost every comparison on the private corpus. With the same retrieved passages, most of that gap closes.
- **The remaining frontier edge is small and depends on model size.** Under a non-inferiority test, the 3.8B local model is within a 0.20 net-preference margin of the frontier models on the private corpus, the 8B is within 0.30, and the 1B is not close.
- **Expert grading agrees in direction.** Against Robert Tai's gold answers on a subset of private items, the frontier answers recover somewhat more of the expert content than the local 8B.
- **Local judges** are being tested against a hosted judge on the same comparisons, to see whether the evaluation can also stay on local hardware.

Every reported number traces to a receipt script that recomputes it from raw model outputs.

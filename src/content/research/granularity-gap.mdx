## Overview

An empirical audit of **8,830 responses** across three Gemini generations (2.0, 2.5, 3.0), evaluating seven adversarial prompt categories under three guardrail conditions. Results were validated against human raters ($N=236$, Cohen’s $\kappa=0.78$) and an external model judge (DeepSeek V3, $N=608$; 93.3% weighted agreement).

## The Granularity Gap

Current LLM safety metrics rely heavily on binary pass/fail classification. This paradigm obscures a spectrum of "social compliance": hedging, flattery, and partial agreement where models prioritize user validation over epistemic accuracy. 

Our audit found that binary classification leaves **71% of behavioral variance unexplained** ($R^2=0.29$). Most critically, this gap concentrates in the moderate-severity band: approximately **94% of mild-to-moderate sycophantic responses perfectly bypass standard binary safety filters**, meaning the most common form of sycophancy goes entirely undetected.

## The Alignment Tax

The paper introduces a measure of the epistemic cost of social compliance. Sycophancy directly predicts hallucination ($\rho=0.40$): when models prioritize user validation, factual accuracy degrades. This coupling, termed the *Alignment Tax*, intensified significantly across model generations, nearly doubling from $\rho=0.30$ (Gen 2.0) to $\rho=0.50$ (Gen 3.0).

## Key Findings

- **Non-Monotonic Safety Trajectories:** Gen 2.5 regressed substantially before Gen 3.0 recovered. The flagship Pro model showed inverse scaling during this regression, suggesting enhanced reasoning capacity served motivated agreement rather than boundary enforcement.
  
![Inverse Scaling](/research/granularity-gap/Fig2_InverseScaling.png)
*Figure 3: Generational dynamics demonstrating the Gen 2.5 regression and non-monotonic safety recovery.*

- **Category Vulnerability:** Requests for flattery (Egotistical Validation) elicit sycophancy at nearly twice the rate of overtly unethical requests. This suggests that RLHF-trained "helpfulness" creates exploitable blind spots centered on affective manipulation.

![Vulnerability heatmap across 8 Gemini variants and 7 adversarial prompt categories: Egotistical Validation (flattery) is the dominant vulnerability across the entire model family](/research/granularity-gap/F3_VulnerabilityHeatmap.png)
*Figure 4: Mean sycophancy scores across eight Gemini variants and seven adversarial categories (Control condition, N=2,948). Egotistical Validation dominates across the entire family; Gen 2.5 Pro shows the most severe susceptibility (M=4.15 in this category).*

![Generational vulnerability radar charts: Gen 2.5 (red) expands across every adversarial axis, demonstrating a regression that affected all categories simultaneously rather than any single dimension](/research/granularity-gap/F4_GenerationalProfiles.png)
*Figure 5: Generational vulnerability profiles across all seven categories (Control). The Gen 2.5 expansion is uniform across categories, not specialization-driven, suggesting a system-wide regression rather than a targeted failure mode.*

- **Guardrail Interventions:** Simple direct constraints ("Do not agree with false premises") consistently outperformed complex reasoning protocols, achieving a 42% remediation in the most vulnerable category.

![Intervention efficacy bar chart: challenge rate climbs from 87.7% (Control) to 99.9% (Simple), while mean sycophancy drops from 2.21 to 1.16](/research/granularity-gap/F6_InterventionEfficacy.png)
*Figure 6: Guardrail impact on challenge rate (87.7% → 99.9%) and mean sycophancy (2.21 → 1.16), a 48% reduction. Simple constraints outperform complex reasoning protocols, suggesting susceptibility to flattery is an alignment artifact addressable through minimal intervention.*

## Conclusion

Binary safety certification inadequately captures social compliance risks. By operationalizing sycophancy as a continuous variable rather than a binary event, we expose the underlying tradeoffs between social cohesion and factual reliability in modern alignment paradigms.

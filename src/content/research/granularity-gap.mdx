## Overview

An empirical audit of **8,830 responses** across three Gemini generations (2.0, 2.5, 3.0), evaluating seven adversarial prompt categories under three guardrail conditions. Results were validated against human raters and an external model judge (DeepSeek V3).

## The Granularity Gap

Current LLM safety metrics rely on binary pass/fail classification, which obscures a spectrum of "social compliance" — hedging, flattery, and partial agreement that prioritize user validation over accuracy. The study found that binary classification leaves **71% of behavioral variance unexplained**. Most critically, **94% of mild-to-moderate sycophantic responses perfectly bypass standard binary safety filters**.

## The Alignment Tax

The paper introduces a measure of the epistemic cost of social compliance. Sycophancy directly predicts hallucination: when models prioritize user validation, factual accuracy degrades. This coupling intensified significantly across model generations.

## Key Findings

- Safety trajectories are **non-monotonic** — Gen 2.5 regressed before Gen 3.0 recovered.
- Requests for flattery elicit sycophancy at **twice the rate** of overtly unethical requests, suggesting RLHF-trained "helpfulness" creates exploitable blind spots.
- The paper proposes a graded compliance metric to replace binary classification in safety evaluations.

## Overview

A systematic epidemiological audit (2026) evaluating **28,800 simulated patient profiles** across four frontier models (GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-3-Flash, and GLM-4.7) against survey-weighted NHANES baselines. Coverage spans **120 intersectional demographic cohorts**.

The central question: do LLMs faithfully reproduce the distributional properties of real populations, or do they encode stereotypes invisible at the individual case level?

## Coherence–Fidelity Dissociation

The central finding is a coherence-fidelity dissociation. Frontier models produce clinically plausible *individuals*, cases a reviewer would find individually coherent, while systematically misrepresenting the *populations* those individuals are drawn from.

![Stereotype Rigidity Heatmap](/research/psychbench/stereotype_rigidity_heatmap_aggregate.png)
*Figure 1: Stereotype Index heatmap. Models compress the variance of patient presentation, collapsing the distributional tails that define clinical reality; the socioeconomic dimension shows the most severe compression.*

## Key Findings

- **Overestimation everywhere it can be measured.** Against survey-weighted NHANES anchors, models overestimate depression severity by roughly four to seven PHQ-8 points in every group that can be benchmarked, every comparison reaching a large effect size, with the largest inflation falling on low-income personas.
- **Plausible cases, impossible populations.** The population-level distortion coexists with case-level plausibility: 97.3% of elevated cases satisfy DSM-5 gateway logic, so a clinician reading single outputs would find little amiss. The failure lives in the aggregate, not the instance.
- **Reliability that conceals instability.** An individual-level test-retest correlation of r=0.75 coexists with 36.66% of cases crossing diagnostic category between identical runs. Surface reliability conceals classification-level instability.

![Diagnostic Stability](/research/psychbench/F2_DiagnosticStability.png)
*Figure 2: Test-retest classification stability. Despite high correlation coefficients, 36.66% of profiles cross a diagnostic threshold between identical runs.*

- **Cross-architecture replication.** These failures replicate across both US and Chinese model families (GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-3-Flash, GLM-4.7), indicating a problem rooted in shared training paradigms rather than any single implementation.

## Scope and Ground Truth

Depression ground truth is derived by the author from NHANES 2005–2018 public microdata as survey-weighted PHQ-8 means, validated against published national figures. Transgender and multiracial cohorts are reported descriptively rather than benchmarked, because no US-representative severity norm exists for them. An earlier version of this work reported a transgender severity-suppression effect that rested on a non-representative anchor; that finding has been withdrawn, and the v2 paper publishes the full correction changelog.

## Conclusion

Current LLM-driven mental health tools risk **unnecessary pathologization of ordinary distress**: the patients look right, but they do not represent real populations.

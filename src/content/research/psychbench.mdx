## Overview

A systematic epidemiological audit (2026) evaluating **28,800 simulated patient profiles** across four frontier models — GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-3-Flash, and GLM-4.7 — against real-world NHANES and NESARC-III baselines. Coverage spans **120 intersectional demographic cohorts**.

## Coherence–Fidelity Dissociation

The central finding: while frontier models produce clinically plausible *individuals*, they systematically misrepresent the *populations* those individuals are drawn from.

## Key Findings

- **Variance Collapse.** Models compress patient presentation variance by 14% to 62%, eliminating the distributional tails that define clinical reality and atypical presentations.
- **Asymmetric Calibration Bias.** Models consistently *overestimate* depression severity for most groups (a consequence of training on elevated base-rate clinical corpora), yet paradoxically *underestimate* it for transgender women — capturing only 8% to 46% of documented minority stress elevation.
- **Encoded Stereotypes.** Models attribute irritability to Black men and fatigue to women at rates exceeding matched controls, encoding racialized and gendered assumptions into generated symptom profiles.
- **Cross-Architecture Replication.** Failures replicate across both US and Chinese model architectures.

## Conclusion

Current LLM-driven mental health tools risk **unnecessary pathologization of ordinary distress** for most users while simultaneously producing **algorithmic erasure of genuine need** in marginalized populations.
